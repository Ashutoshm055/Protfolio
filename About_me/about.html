Research Interest

Cloud Computing & Distributed Systems Optimization

Cloud computing is my primary research passion, and it stems from a deep fascination with how we can build systems that are not just powerful, but conscientious. Over the past few years, I've investigated critical challenges in cloud infrastructure:

Task Scheduling & Workload Prediction: I've designed algorithms to intelligently route jobs through cloud systems, reducing latency and cost. My work explores how machine learning and metaheuristic algorithms, from Whale Optimization to nature-inspired swarm intelligence, can predict workload patterns and make real-time scheduling decisions that adapt to changing demands.

Load Balancing & Virtualization: I've developed hybrid optimization frameworks that balance computational load across data centers while respecting energy constraints. Through reinforcement learning-assisted approaches, I've achieved significant improvements in SLA compliance and energy efficiency on real-world Google and Alibaba cluster traces.

Distributed Systems & Orchestration: My research dives into how distributed systems can be orchestrated at scale, ensuring consistency, reliability, and resilience across geographically dispersed resources. The engineering challenges here are profound: how do we index spatiotemporal data at high throughput? How do we manage resource allocation across hybrid classical-quantum environments?

But what truly excites me is the sustainability dimension. Cloud data centers consume immense energy, and I'm driven by the vision of Green Cloud Computing, where every optimization I design directly translates to reduced carbon footprint and environmental responsibility. This isn't just an engineering goal; it's a moral imperative.

Geospatial Big Data Analytics

Beyond traditional cloud computing, I've developed a strong interest in geospatial big data, the convergence of location-aware data, environmental monitoring, and cloud-native analytics.

My research here focuses on real-time environmental sensing and analysis. I've built cloud-native digital twins that fuse open geospatial datasets with real-time sensor streams to enable near-instant decision support. For instance, my work on urban flood risk assessment processes hundreds of geospatial layers in sub-second response times, delivering actionable intelligence to city planners and emergency responders. I've also explored applications in civil and marine infrastructure monitoring, where ML-driven damage prediction combined with geospatial analytics can preemptively identify risks before they become critical.

What captivates me is how cloud platforms enable us to process planetary-scale environmental data in real time, turning raw sensor feeds and satellite imagery into insights that can save lives and protect ecosystems.

Digital Twins: Bridging Physical & Digital Realities

Digital twins represent a frontier where cloud computing, machine learning, and IoT converge. I'm fascinated by the question: How can we create faithful digital replicas of physical systems that not only monitor but actively decide and act?

My research explores cloud-hosted digital twins for critical infrastructure, from civil engineering structures to marine installations. These aren't passive monitoring systems; they're decision-intelligent frameworks that combine sensor fusion, anomaly detection, and metaheuristic optimization to predict failures, optimize maintenance schedules, and reduce response latency during emergencies. The beauty of hosting these on cloud platforms is the elasticity: as monitoring demands grow, the system scales seamlessly.

Machine Learning: The Enabler of Everything

If I had to distill my research philosophy into one statement, it would be: Machine learning is the technology that opens doors when we're blocked by the constraints of our physical systems.

Whether it's predicting quantum job suitability, forecasting cloud workloads, detecting infrastructure damage, or learning adaptive resource allocation policies, machine learning is the common thread. Even in scenarios where classical algorithms reach their limits, ML approaches like adaptive learning, reinforcement learning, and hybrid decision intelligence break through. When cloud resources are constrained or digital twins have incomplete sensor data, machine learning fills the gaps, learns from uncertainty, and makes intelligent decisions despite imperfect information.

This is why I integrate ML into nearly every research project: it's not a separate interest, but the fundamental multiplier that amplifies the impact of every system I design.

Beyond Academics

I believe that curiosity is a 24/7 endeavor, and for me, the boundaries between "academics" and "life" have blurred beautifully.

I'm an avid reader, books are my refuge and my teacher. There's something magical about stepping into someone else's perspective, whether through fiction, science, philosophy, or research papers. I love to dance and sing; they're my creative outlets when my mind needs to escape the world of algorithms and architectures. But if I'm being completely truthful, the thing that energizes me most is research and discovery. It's not relegated to my lab time or office hours; it's become how I live. Every day brings new questions: Can we do this faster? Could that system work differently? What if we approached this problem from an entirely new angle? Finding answers or discovering entirely new questions, is genuinely my favorite thing to do. It's not work to me; it's living.

I don't find myself drawn to competitive sports or large social gatherings. My idea of a perfect day is diving deep into a challenging problem, emerging with new insights, and sharing those discoveries with people who care about making technology better. That blend of curiosity, continuous learning, and the pursuit of meaningful impact defines who I am both inside and outside the classroom.
